# Wave 1 / Track 3: Other art styles (data and generalization strategy)

Status: research notes, 2026-11-18. Author of the project: Hoopakid.
Scope: (1) a list of permissively licensed, animated pixel-art character packs for an out-of-style (OOS) test set and extra training data; (2) generalization strategies, with evidence; (3) how to measure generalization honestly.

Conventions:
- **VERIFIED** means the license was read on the original page during this research, and the quote is copied from that page or file.
- **UNVERIFIED** means the claim comes from a secondary source, a commenter, my own inference, or memory, and needs checking before use.
- Sprite sizes, frame counts and directions are only listed as verified when the author's page states them. Everything else should be checked against the files before ingestion.
- OpenGameArt (OGA) pages blocked the WebFetch tool (HTTP 403). They were read through a plain HTTP GET, and the "License(s)" field and description were extracted from the page HTML.

---

## 1. Summary of findings

- There are enough **CC0 / CC-BY** animated character packs to build a meaningful OOS test set covering four axes: (a) top-down chibi/big-head at 16 px, (b) top-down retro, palette-limited at 16x24, (c) side-view platformer at 16 to 32 px, and (d) large side-view "HD pixel" fighters at about 100 to 200 px canvases.
- Few permissive packs share LPC's exact contract (top-down, 4 directions, walk plus attack). The best matches are **Ninja Adventure** (CC0, 16 px, 4-direction), **Puny Characters** (CC0, 8-direction, 32x32 canvas), **Chasersgaming RPG Characters** (CC0, 4-direction, 16x24), **Antifarea's Twelve 16x18 RPG sprites** (CC-BY 3.0, 4-direction walk) and **Bit Era Game Character - Extended** (CC0, front/right/back walk plus attack).
- Many popular itch.io packs are **not usable**. Their terms forbid redistribution and/or AI training: Tiny RPG Character pack (Zerie), Mystic Woods, Sunnyside World, Pipoya, CraftPix (explicit AI-training ban). Anokolisa's terms could not be read.
- **Mixamo is out**: its terms explicitly forbid ML training on any derived content. **Quaternius** (CC0) characters and animations, the **Superpowers** 3D character (CC0), **CMU mocap** ("may be copied, modified, or redistributed without permission") and **100STYLE** (CC BY 4.0) are permissive sources for a synthetic 3D-to-pixel pipeline.
- Published evidence on pixel-art pose translation shows that (i) models trained on **modular, part-composed** datasets (like LPC) mostly memorize parts and fail on unique characters, and (ii) **training on a larger, style-diverse set improved results** even on the single-style validation sets ([Coutinho & Chaimowicz 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06413); summarized in [Coutinho & Chaimowicz 2024, arXiv 2409.10721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10721)). This directly supports the risk that moviepix overfits to LPC.

## 3. Generalization strategies (evidence, cost and benefit)

Context: moviepix copies reference pixels to new positions, so its "style" exposure is mostly (a) silhouette/proportion priors, (b) where the limbs and parts are, (c) canvas size and pivot conventions, and (d) the motion vocabulary (LPC timing and poses). Palette shifts matter less for a pixel-copying model than for a colour-generating one. Shape, proportion and part layout are the real risk.

### 3.1 Evidence from the literature

- **Part-composed datasets lead to memorization.** On Tiny Hero (an LPC-derived, modular dataset), output was "perceptually identical to the ground truth", but the authors note this "can only be achieved due to how the Tiny Hero dataset was built ... This test does not show that the proposed model can generalize the translation but can memorize segments of the sprites it sees during training." Non-modular RPG Maker XP sprites had the worst FID (9.49 vs 0.115). Source: [Coutinho & Chaimowicz 2022, arXiv 2208.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06413), Sec. V-A, Table II. *This is exactly moviepix's situation with LPC.*
- **Diversity helps even in-style.** "They also assembled a diverse dataset with 14k paired images ... and observed that training with much more data yielded better results when validating with the more artistically cohesive individual datasets" ([arXiv 2409.10721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10721), related work, summarizing [Graphical Models 132, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.GMOD.2024.101213)). Caution: that 14k set was scraped and includes RPG Maker art, so moviepix **cannot reuse it**, only the lesson. They used hue rotation as augmentation.
- **Palette-index representations overfit; a histogram loss helps slightly**: "While the palette representation led to much worse results due to overfitting, penalizing the generator for using colors with a different histogram than the one from the input image yielded slightly improved images" (summary of Coutinho & Chaimowicz, AIIDE 2022, https://doi.org/10.1609/AIIDE.V18I1.21951, in [arXiv 2409.10721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10721)). moviepix's copy-based design already sidesteps colour drift, which is a strength.
- **The alpha channel helps shape fidelity**: models trained with RGBA "generate shapes with higher fidelity to the ground truth and avoid dangling pixels" ([arXiv 2208.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06413), Sec. V-B).
- **Off-the-shelf pose estimators fail on sprites**: "existing pose estimation models struggle with game sprites", and DW-Pose/OpenPose "do not generalize well" to sprite illustrations, so the authors labelled poses by hand ([Sprite Sheet Diffusion, arXiv 2412.03685](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03685v2)). Their out-of-sample test was only 12 sequences, with weak fine details (hair, props). This is a warning on skeleton-based approaches and on tiny test sets.
- **Keypoint and motion models are category-bound**: FOMM learns keypoints plus local affine transforms without annotations, but is trained per object category ([Siarohin et al. 2019, arXiv 2003.00196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00196); code MIT, https://github.com/AliaksandrSiarohin/first-order-model). Generalization comes from in-category diversity, not from the architecture alone.
- **Skeleton rigging of single drawings works for humanoid figures**: Animated Drawings rigs a single drawing of a human figure and retargets BVH motion ([arXiv 2303.12741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12741); code MIT, https://github.com/facebookresearch/AnimatedDrawings). It is front-view, human-figure-only and relies on a keypoint detector; sprites at 16 to 32 px are much harder (see the pose-estimator evidence above).
- **Anime character pose transfer needs a custom dense pose representation**: CoNR uses "Ultra-Dense Pose" because anime proportions and clothing break SMPL-style body models, and it improves with more reference views ([arXiv 2207.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05378), IJCAI 2023).
- General ML evidence (well-established, cited for mechanism only): domain randomization lets a sim-trained model transfer to real images ([Tobin et al. 2017, arXiv 1703.06907](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06907)); differentiable augmentation stabilizes limited-data training ([DiffAugment, arXiv 2006.10738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10738)); few-shot subject adaptation by fine-tuning ([DreamBooth, arXiv 2208.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242); [LoRA, arXiv 2106.09685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)); test-time training on the test input ([Sun et al., arXiv 1909.13231](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13231)).

### 3.2 Ranked strategies (best cost/benefit first)

1. **Structured LPC augmentation using LPC's own layer structure** (cost: low; benefit: high for proportions).
   - LPC characters are composed from layers (body, head, hair, clothing), so proportions can be warped *consistently across all frames of an action*: enlarge the head/hair layers (chibi, 1.3 to 2x), shorten legs or torso, widen the body, scale the whole character to 24 to 48 px tall within the 64 px canvas, and shift the pivot. Because the target frames receive the same transform, ground truth stays exact. This is the cheapest way to break the "LPC proportions" prior.
   - Add **nearest-neighbour rescale** (0.5x to 32x32, with targets transformed identically) so 32x32 inputs are in-distribution, rather than relying on upsampling at inference.
   - **Outline variants**: add a 1 px outer outline (black, darkest palette colour, or selective outline), or remove an existing outline. Apply identically to reference and targets.
   - **Palette remap / hue rotation** (cheap, used by Coutinho & Chaimowicz). Mostly helps any colour-dependent matching inside the model.
   - **Shading variants**: flatten shading (map ramps to a single colour), posterize to 2 to 4 colours (GB/NES look), or add dithering. These simulate the Chasersgaming and Tiny 16 styles.
   - **Horizontal flip with left/right label swap**: generates mirrored training pairs. Caveat: LPC left and right sheets are already mirror-consistent for most layers, so the gain is limited. Flipping is still important for side-only packs (0x72, LuizMelo, Calciumtrice), whose ground truth exists for one facing only.
   - Caveat: augmentation cannot invent new *motion styles* (e.g. a platformer run with big squash/stretch). That needs real or synthetic data (strategies 2 and 3).
2. **Add real, permissive non-LPC packs to training, holding packs out for testing** (cost: low to medium, mostly slicing and labelling; benefit: high).
   - Candidates for training: Superpowers Ninja Adventure / Prehistoric / RPG-battle sets (CC0), Puny Characters (CC0), Bit Era (CC0), Ars Notoria layered hero (CC0), LuizMelo (CC0, side view), Pixel Frog (CC0).
   - Use strict **leave-one-pack-out**: never put frames of the same pack, or of the same artist where possible (e.g. Pixel-Boy across Ninja Adventure itch and Superpowers), into both training and test.
   - Main cost: a per-pack adapter mapping sheet layout to (action, direction, frame index, pivot), plus a mapping of non-LPC actions onto moviepix's action vocabulary (e.g. "run" to walk, "sword attack" to slash).
3. **Few-shot per-user adaptation (test-time fine-tuning)** (cost: low for the project, about seconds to minutes per user on CPU/GPU for a 4.5M model; benefit: high for users who already have 1 to 2 animated frames or a similar character).
   - Self-supervised variant with no ground truth: create pseudo-pairs from the user's sprite through augmentation (flip, small affine moves of parts) and fine-tune briefly. This is in the spirit of test-time training (arXiv 1909.13231).
   - Supervised variant: the user provides one finished animation of character A, and the model is adapted to that style before animating characters B, C and so on in the same style. This is a realistic indie workflow.
   - Measure it with the protocol in Section 4 (k-shot curves).
4. **Synthetic 3D-to-pixel data** (cost: medium to high; benefit: medium to high, mainly for motion and viewpoint diversity, and for **exact dense correspondence labels**).
   - Pipeline: Quaternius CC0 characters plus Quaternius UAL CC0 animations (optionally CMU mocap or 100STYLE retargeted). Render in Blender with an orthographic 3/4 top-down camera at 4 directions (and pure side view), flat/toon shading, nearest-neighbour downsample to 32 or 64 px, palette quantization (k-means to 8 to 24 colours), optional outline pass.
   - Big advantage for a pixel-copying model: per-pixel **ground-truth correspondence** (render UV or vertex IDs alongside colour), which hand-drawn packs never provide. This also allows varied proportions (scale bones and head) and arbitrary actions.
   - Risks: a "3D-render look" domain gap (smooth rotations and no animator's pose exaggeration), and engineering time. Mixamo is excluded (AI/ML clause, including testing). Check each Quaternius pack page individually, since the free tier is a subset.
5. **Explicit part segmentation / skeleton conditioning** (cost: high; benefit: uncertain at 16 to 32 px).
   - LPC layers give free part masks for training a part segmenter. A segmenter that transfers to other styles could make a part-wise warping model style-agnostic.
   - Evidence is weak at sprite scale: pose estimators fail on sprites (arXiv 2412.03685), and CoNR needed a custom dense representation. Defer until strategies 1, 2 and 4 are measured.
6. **Style-agnostic input representations** (cost: low to medium; benefit: unclear). Examples: feed palette-index or luminance-only maps, or alpha plus edge maps, for correspondence, then copy original colours. Caution: palette-index *outputs* overfit in a GAN setting (AIIDE 2022, via arXiv 2409.10721). As *inputs* to a correspondence model this is untested. Treat it as an ablation, not a plan.

## 4. Measuring generalization honestly

### 4.1 Test-set design
- **Splits** (report each separately, never pooled):
  - (a) In-distribution LPC held-out characters, with **held-out layer combinations and held-out individual assets** (e.g. hairstyles or armours never seen in training). A held-out character made of seen parts tests memorization, not generalization; see the Tiny Hero result.
  - (b) OOS-near: top-down 4-direction packs (Ninja Adventure, Puny, Chasersgaming, Antifarea, Bit Era).
  - (c) OOS-far: side view (Calciumtrice, 0x72, Pixel Frog, LuizMelo, Sunny Land), non-humanoids (Kings and Pigs, DawnLike creatures), large canvases (LuizMelo).
  - (d) Pixelized vector characters (GameArt2D freebies, Chibi Swordman) as a separate "synthetic-style" split.
- **Leave-one-pack-out (and leave-one-artist-out)** whenever non-LPC packs are also used for training. Report the pack-level mean and the worst pack, not just the mean.
- **Stratify by axis**: size (16, 24, 32, 48, 64, >64 px), view (top-down vs side), proportion (head-to-body ratio bins), outline (yes/no), palette size (≤4, 5 to 16, >16), action and direction.
- **Size of the test set**: aim for at least 30 characters per axis bucket where data allows. Sprite Sheet Diffusion's 12-sequence OOS test shows how weak tiny tests are. Publish the manifest and checksums, and freeze it before tuning (keep a separate dev split for choosing models).
- **Action and timing alignment**: packs differ in frame counts (e.g. LuizMelo run 8 frames, LPC walk differs). Define a canonical action phase (0 to 1), compare by **phase-resampled** nearest frames, or score each frame against the closest ground-truth frame within a phase window. Document every action mapping (e.g. "Puny Sword Attack" to "slash").
- **Pivot and canvas alignment**: normalize by a foot/pivot anchor and the bounding box before scoring. Otherwise metrics punish legitimate placement differences.

### 4.2 Metrics (pixel-art-appropriate)
- **Palette exactness**: the share of output opaque pixels whose RGBA exactly matches a colour in the input sprite. moviepix should score about 100% by design. Report it anyway, since it will catch regressions.
- **Alpha/silhouette IoU** per frame (shape correctness; the most style-sensitive signal).
- **Foreground exact-pixel accuracy** and **foreground L1** after anchor alignment, plus a **shift-tolerant** variant (best match within ±1 px) so 1 px offsets are not over-penalized.
- **Temporal consistency**: frame-to-frame silhouette IoU stability, and loop closure (first vs last frame for cyclic actions).
- **Identity preservation**: colour-histogram distance between the reference and each output frame (cheap). Optionally DINO-feature similarity as in VBench, used by arXiv 2412.03685, but deep features on 16 px sprites are of doubtful validity, so treat them as secondary.
- **Avoid FID** on these small sets. Even Coutinho & Chaimowicz note that FID "should be calculated with thousands of images" ([arXiv 2208.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06413), footnote 2).
- **Baselines** (essential for honesty): (1) copy the reference frame unchanged for every frame; (2) a rigid bounce/translate heuristic; (3) for left/right, a mirrored ground-truth frame where available. Report the model's gain over these.
- **Human evaluation**: a blinded 2AFC (model vs baseline, or model vs ground truth) with pixel artists on a fixed random subset per split. Report the win rate with confidence intervals.
- **Calibration and failure taxonomy**: tag failures (limb dropped, head distorted, wrong direction, outline broken, palette leak, jitter) per split. This makes "where it breaks" visible rather than averaged away.

## 5. Risks
- **License drift**: itch.io pages can change terms (the Puny Characters license field was corrected after a commenter flagged it, per search results on https://merchant-shade.itch.io/16x16-puny-characters/comments). Archive the page text (e.g. Wayback snapshot links) and the date of verification for every pack in the manifest.
- **Mixed-provenance bundles**: Brackeys credits art from other packs. Verify upstream licenses before training on it.
- **Share-alike (CC-BY-SA, GPL)**: LPC itself includes CC-BY-SA/GPL assets. This is out of scope for this track, but the same question (does SA reach the weights?) applies to any SA pack added here. The safest course is CC0/CC-BY only.
- **Action-vocabulary mismatch** makes OOS ground truth only "one valid answer". Shift-tolerant and silhouette metrics plus human evaluation mitigate this.
- **Test contamination**: the same artist across packs (Pixel-Boy in Ninja Adventure and Superpowers; Pixel Frog across packs). Hold out by artist.
- **Synthetic domain gap**: 3D renders may teach unpixel-like motion (sub-pixel jitter, rotation blur). Quantize and snap, and always validate on real packs.
